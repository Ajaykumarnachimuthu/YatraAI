### 1. Project Mission & Problem Statement
Planning intercity travel across India is structurally fragmented. Travellers must navigate disparate reservation portals (IRCTC for railways, private and state airline platforms, regional road networks) without an integrated platform capable of evaluating multi-modal alternatives. Crucially, existing platforms fail to personalize recommendations based on individual preferences across competing journey dimensions: monetary cost, door-to-door transit time, operational delay risk, cabin comfort, schedule convenience, and environmental carbon footprint.

**Yātrā AI** is designed as an intelligent travel orchestration and personalization engine that evaluates multi-modal travel itineraries across India and predicts whether an itinerary matches a traveller's unique behavioral profile.

### 2. The Hybrid Data Architecture
To overcome the complete absence of publicly accessible, privacy-compliant Indian multi-modal booking transactions, Yātrā AI pioneered a **Hybrid Data Strategy**:

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                            YĀTRĀ AI HYBRID FOUNDATION                       |
+------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
| REAL / CANONICAL OPERATIONAL DATA  | SYNTHETIC BEHAVIOURAL DATA             |
| (100% Grounded in Public Sources)  | (Generated via Calibrated Simulation)  |
+------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
| * 8,990 Railway Stations (JSON)   | * 5,000 Traveller Profiles             |
| * 5,208 Active Trains (11,100+ raw)| * 7-D Continuous Travel DNA Vectors    |
| * 300,259 Domestic Flight Fares    | * 40,000 Multi-Modal Search Sessions   |
| * Historical Rail Delay Statistics | * 138,603 Multinomial Discrete Choices |
| * IMD High-Resolution Rainfall     | * Target: 'chosen' in {0, 1} (MNL Sim) |
+------------------------------------+----------------------------------------+
```

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Strict Scientific Boundary**:  
> The supervised learning experiment evaluates the ability of machine learning models to recover synthetic behavioural choice patterns generated under explicitly documented simulation assumptions. The choice target (`chosen`) represents **synthetic behavioural ground truth** generated via a Multinomial Logit (MNL) discrete choice model. It must **not** be characterized as observed real-world booking transactions.

### 3. Machine Learning Formulation & Leakage-Safe Partitioning
- **Primary Supervised Task**: Binary classification predicting whether an itinerary alternative within a search session is selected by the traveller (`chosen = 1`) or rejected (`chosen = 0`).
- **Dataset Scale**: **138,603 itinerary rows** across **40,000 search sessions** generated by **5,000 unique travellers** (mean $3.465$ candidate alternatives per session).
- **Class Distribution**: 40,000 chosen (28.86%) vs. 98,603 not chosen (71.14%), creating a natural **2.465 : 1 class imbalance** reflecting exactly one choice among 2 to 5 options per session.
- **Leakage-Safe Partitioning**: Partitioned strictly by `traveller_id` (Seed 42) into **70% Train** (3,500 travellers / 97,163 rows), **15% Validation** (750 travellers / 20,715 rows), and **15% Test** (750 travellers / 20,725 rows). Zero traveller or session overlap across partitions.
- **Exclusion of Post-Choice Derivatives**: Mathematical and empirical audits verified that `choice_probability`, `utility`, `rank`, and entity IDs were strictly excluded. Derived candidate scores (`cost_score`, `time_score`, `carbon_score`, etc.) were proven to be **strictly pre-choice attributes** normalized relative to session bounds prior to choice simulation.

### 4. Key Experimental Results & Model Selection

Four distinct model families were evaluated across **Core (Model A — 28 features)** and **Extended (Model B — 29 features, incorporating `persona_type`)** configurations:

```
Baseline Test ROC-AUC Comparison:
Logistic Regression (Core):  [======              ] 0.6820
Decision Tree (Core):        [=====               ] 0.6700
Random Forest (Core):        [=======             ] 0.6979
Gradient Boosting (Core):    [=======             ] 0.6976
```

#### The Decision-Threshold Optimization Breakthrough:
At the canonical threshold ($\tau = 0.50$), all models exhibited a severe recall deficit (predicting positive on only ~7% to 12% of test candidates). By conducting a systematic decision-threshold sweep ($\tau \in [0.20, 0.60]$) **strictly on the Validation partition**, the optimal operating threshold was determined to be **$\mathbf{\tau^* = 0.30}$**.

| Metric | Baseline Default ($\tau = 0.50$) | Champion Model Tuned ($\tau^* = 0.30$) | Net Shift | Operational Impact |
|---|:---:|:---:|:---:|---|
| **Test F1 Score** | 0.3366 | **0.5116** | **+0.1750 (+52.0%)** | Optimal harmonic precision/recall trade-off |
| **Test Recall** | 23.82% | **62.65%** | **+38.83% pts** | Captures nearly 2 out of 3 desired itineraries |
| **Test Precision** | 57.37% | **43.23%** | -14.14% pts | Tolerates controlled, plausible consideration options |
| **Test ROC-AUC** | 0.6976 | **0.6976** | 0.0000 | Threshold-invariant ranking discrimination |
| **Test Accuracy** | 72.82% | **65.37%** | -7.45% pts | Trade-off: accepts higher FP to eliminate fatal FN |
| **True Positives (TP)** | 1,429 | **3,759** | **+2,330 (+163%)** | Engine surfaces 2,330 additional preferred journeys |
| **False Negatives (FN)** | 4,571 | **2,241** | **-2,330 (-51.0%)** | Missed preferred journeys cut by more than half |

#### Final Recommended Champion:
**`Gradient Boosting Core (GB_core)` at Decision Threshold $\mathbf{\tau^* = 0.30}$**.  
Selected based on the combined evaluation of validation performance, test generalization ($\text{Test F1} = 0.5116$), low overfitting gap ($\Delta \text{AUC}_{\text{train-test}} = 0.0235$), ultra-low inference latency (< 1.5 MB footprint), and architectural parsimony (28 features, operating directly on continuous Travel DNA without requiring a discrete persona classifier).

### 5. Primary Scientific & Engineering Insights

1. **Non-Linear Interactions Outperform Linear Main Effects**:  
   Tree-based models substantially outperform Logistic Regression (+47.2% relative F1 gain in baseline). In discrete choice theory, systematic utility is bilinear ($\beta \cdot \text{DNA} \cdot \text{Score}$). Tree structures naturally recover these nested conditional interactions, which linear models cannot capture without manual polynomial expansion.
2. **`persona_type` Provides Negligible Incremental Signal**:  
   Adding categorical persona archetypes yielded virtually zero improvement ($\Delta \text{AUC} \le 0.0005$, $\Delta \text{F1} \le 0.0032$, feature importance < 0.81%). Continuous Travel DNA vectors already provide complete, fine-grained psychometric coordinates; persona archetypes serve purely as generative clustering priors.
3. **Relative Choice-Set Scores Dominate Raw Physical Attributes**:  
   Normalized candidate scores account for **51.96%** of total model importance compared to **23.01%** for raw attributes. Traveller discrete choice is governed by relative advantages within the immediate choice set, rather than absolute fares or durations that vary arbitrarily across geographic corridors.
4. **Generalization Integrity**:  
   Validation F1 (0.5283) generalized to Test F1 (0.5116) with only a 3.1% delta, verifying zero leakage across unseen travellers.

---

### 6. Architectural Limitations & Production Roadmap
- **Pointwise Binary Independence**: The model evaluates each alternative independently, without enforcing the session-level constraint that exactly one alternative is selected from the candidate set ($\sum_{j \in C_n} Y_{nj} = 1$).
- **Future Direction**: The natural next step for Yātrā AI is to evolve this binary recommendation filter into a **Session-Aware Learning-to-Rank framework (LightGBM Ranker / LambdaMART)** optimizing listwise metrics (NDCG@1, MRR) and integrating live transit APIs.
